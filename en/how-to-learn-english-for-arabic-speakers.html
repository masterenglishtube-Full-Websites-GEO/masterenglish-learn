How to Learn English as an Arabic Speaker: A Free Step-by-Step Plan

Master English · أتقن الإنجليزيةUpdated 23 October 2026

Most Arabic speakers who “studied English for years” don't lack information. They lack order: a lesson here, a channel there, and no clear next step. This plan puts the steps in order, and every step is free.

Short answer. 1) Find your real level with a free placement test. 2) Follow one learning path for that level, in order. 3) Fix the sounds and mistakes Arabic speakers make, early. 4) Speak out loud from the first day, ten minutes a day. 5) After intermediate level (B1), train your ear with real, fast English.

Step 1: Find your real level

Starting too low is boring; starting too high makes you quit. The free placement test (25 questions, explained in Arabic) places you from A1 to C1, shows your strong and weak skills, and emails you a personal list of lessons in order.

Step 3: Fix the Arabic-speaker problems early

Arabic speakers tend to make the same mistakes: P and B, the two TH sounds, the American R, “I am agree”, “discuss about”, missing -s. Fixing them early is much easier than unlearning them later. Start with the guide to the English sounds that are hard for Arabic speakers and the common mistakes guide (wrong sentence, right sentence, and why).

Step 4: Speak out loud from day one

Understanding and speaking are different skills. Reading and watching build understanding; only saying sentences out loud builds speaking. Ten minutes a day is enough to start: say every example sentence out loud, describe what you are doing in simple English (the 5-minute “think in English” exercise), and practice alone first so fear of mistakes doesn't slow you down. The free speaking practice uses your microphone to check words like pen and Ben.

Step 5: Train your ear with real English

Around B1, textbook English stops being enough: real speakers swallow letters, link words and say “gonna”. Learn the four sound changes in why Arabic speakers don't understand fast English and movies, then add an English-only channel (see the best YouTube channels for Arabic speakers).

FAQ

Can Arabic speakers learn English for free?

Yes. Master English offers a free placement test, free learning paths by level and goal, guides, and 1,200+ free video lessons explained in Arabic. Paid courses are optional, for learners who want one goal covered in order.

Where should an Arabic speaker start learning English?

Start by finding your level with a placement test, then follow one path for that level in order: the beginner path for A1 to A2, the intermediate path for B1 to B2, or the advanced path for C1.

How long does it take to learn English?

It depends on your starting level and how regularly you practice. Short daily practice, out loud, beats long irregular sessions; most learners notice clear progress within weeks when they follow one path every day.

Should I learn American or British English?

Either works; pick one model for pronunciation and stay consistent. Master English teaches American pronunciation, and explains the differences in its American vs British accent lesson.
